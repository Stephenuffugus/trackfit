# Trackfit — the founder story

*Use this as the site "About" page, the long-form forum post, or the
narrative in any writeup. It's true; keep it true. ~600 words.*

---

It started as a gift.

A friend of mine has been in model railroading longer than I've been
alive. He's the kind of hobbyist this whole pastime is built on —
patient, particular, happiest on the basement floor with a pile of
sectional track and an evening ahead of him. And every time I visited,
he was stuck on the same small thing: a gap between two pieces of laid
track, and the question of which combination of straights and curves
would actually close it. Not a hard problem for a computer. A genuinely
annoying problem for a person on their knees with a tape measure.

So I built him something. You tell it what's in your box, you measure the
gap, and it tells you every combination that fits. And — the part I cared
about most — when *nothing* in your box fits exactly, it doesn't just shrug.
It tells you the one piece you'd need: "a 2.5-inch straight closes this
exactly." That sentence is the whole point. It's the most useful thing
anyone can tell you in that moment, and no tool was saying it.

It grew. A real track library — sixteen systems across N, HO, OO, and O,
every piece carrying the source I pulled it from and the date I checked
it. A way to photograph a gap with a quarter or a dollar bill in frame
and have it read the length. A "what can I build with what I already
own?" screen, because that's the other question every one of us asks. A
way to import an AnyRail plan and get back a shopping list of just what
you're missing. It quietly turned into a real tool.

Here's the honest part. I built it to put in front of him and his
friends, and my first tester drifted off before he really used it. That
happens. But it left me with a tool that works, that I use, and that I
think a lot of you would use too — and no good reason to keep it on a
shelf waiting for one person. So I'm putting it out.

**Trackfit is free.** The solver, the full track library, your inventory,
the layout matcher, the gap measurement, the printable cut templates —
free, and staying free. There's an optional one-time **$19 Founder**
unlock for the extras: a printable insurance-grade inventory binder, the
full dealer list, and photo-identification of pieces as that rolls out.
One time. No subscription — this hobby buys its software once and I'm not
going to be the person who changes that. Founders lock in $19; if the
price goes up later, theirs doesn't.

I'm not going to pretend it's finished or that every number in the
library has been triple-checked against a manufacturer's latest
catalog — that re-verification is ongoing, and I'd rather tell you that
than oversell it. What I will promise is this: if you find a track length
that's wrong, tell me, and I'll fix it that week. I read the forums. I
answer. The fact that a person is actually behind this and listening is
not a marketing line — in a hobby full of abandoned software, it's the
entire difference.

If it saves you one frustrated afternoon on the basement floor, it did
its job. If it does that often enough that you want to throw $19 at the
person who made it, that's how this stays alive and gets better.

That's the whole story. Lay it out before you lay it down.

— Stephen
